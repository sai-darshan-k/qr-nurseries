Plant doctor

Read my signs

What your ZZ is trying to tell you.

Overwatering. The rhizomes are sitting in wet soil. Stop watering, let the soil dry completely, and check for mushy roots.
Fix: dry out, then water half as often
Root rot. Unpot, cut away soft brown rhizomes with clean scissors, and repot healthy parts into fresh, dry mix.
Fix: trim, repot, wait 7 days to water
Underwatering. Even ZZ has limits. Give it a deep soak until water runs out the bottom, then drain well.
Fix: one deep drink, then back to routine
Too little light. It's surviving, not thriving. Move it closer to a window and rotate the pot a quarter turn each month.
Fix: brighter spot and regular rotation
Scorch or chill. Direct afternoon sun burns the leaves. Cold drafts and AC blasts below 12°C do the same.
Fix: move away from hot glass and cold vents
Growing together

What to expect

Week 1–2

Settling in

Little visible change while it adjusts to your light. Don't worry, and don't water yet.

Month 2–4

First new shoot

A spear-like stem pushes up from the soil and unfurls into bright, lime-green leaves.

Month 6–12

Fuller and glossier

New leaves darken to deep emerald. Expect 2–4 new stems a year in good light.

Year 2–3

Time to repot or divide

Split the rhizomes to make a second plant, a great gift for a friend.

Did you know?

Fun facts about me

01

I'm from Eastern Africa, where I survive long dry seasons by storing water in my underground rhizomes.

02

My shine is natural. A waxy coating on my leaves locks in moisture, so I never need leaf shine sprays.

03

A single fallen leaf, pushed into soil, can grow roots and a brand-new rhizome. It's slow, but it works.

04

I'm nicknamed the "Eternity Plant" because with basic care I can live happily for over a decade.

Good to know: not for nibbling

All parts contain calcium oxalate crystals. If chewed, they can irritate the mouth and stomach of pets and people.

  • Place on a shelf or high stand
  • Wash hands after pruning or repotting
  • If eaten, rinse the mouth and call your vet or doctor
